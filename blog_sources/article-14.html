What We Learned at AltaLab — and Where We're Pointed

November 2026 · Eric Kinney

A month ago, we completed the AltaLab accelerator cohort. It was one of the more clarifying experiences of the company's early life — not because it changed our fundamental thesis, but because it sharpened everything around it.

Here's what we came in with, and what we left with.

What We Came In With

Scurry is building compliance-first stablecoin payment infrastructure for the U.S.-Mexico corridor. FinCEN-registered. A BSA program that was operational before our first user. An AI compliance agent — Murry — whose hardcoded controls were designed around the specific fraud risks of AI-initiated financial transactions. A founding team with thirty years of BSA Officer experience and an engineering approach built around controls that cannot fail quietly.

The core thesis: the U.S.-Mexico payment corridor is expensive, slow, and operationally painful for the businesses that depend on it — and the compliance infrastructure that should serve those businesses has never been built correctly from the ground up.

That thesis didn't change.

What We Sharpened

The accelerator pushed us to be more specific about who we're building for and why.

The answer: B2B operators in the U.S.-Mexico corridor. Businesses with real cross-border payment needs — supplier payments, multi-currency treasury management, working capital flows for operators on both sides of the border. Companies that are currently paying too much, waiting too long, and managing too much operational complexity to move money across a border that their business depends on.

This is not the consumer remittance market. Consumer remittance is a large, competitive, well-served market with strong incumbents. Our customers are not sending money home. They are running a business, and moving money across the U.S.-Mexico border is a core operational function — not an occasional transaction.

The distinction matters because the compliance requirements are different, the transaction sizes are different, the frequency is different, and the value we create is different. A business that runs 40 cross-border supplier payments a month does not have the same problem as a consumer sending $200 to family. Murry is built for the former.

What Comes Next

We are in the stretch run of our pre-seed raise, and we are in active customer discovery conversations with businesses in the corridor. The picture those conversations are painting — of operational complexity, compliance uncertainty, and cost structures that don't reflect what the underlying payment infrastructure should cost — is exactly the market we built Scurry to serve.

The accelerator cohort is done. The work it pointed us toward is just getting started.
