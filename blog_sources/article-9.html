The EarnIn Case: Why Credit Equity Is the Next Frontier for Stablecoin Platforms

September 2026 · Eric Kinney

On August 27, 2026, Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser filed a lawsuit against EarnIn the earned wage access fintech that markets itself as "no interest, no hidden fees" alleging APRs of 388% on average and over 1,000% in individual cases.

The numbers in the complaint deserve to be read slowly.

Between January 2023 and July 2025, EarnIn made more than 3.1 million loans to 56,778 Colorado consumers, lending approximately $300 million and collecting more than $16 million in tips and "Lightning Speed" fees. Consumers were charged a fee on more than 92% of transactions.

One Colorado consumer took out 1,151 loans, paying $4,038.50 in Lightning Speed fees at an average APR of 1,421%.

Another took out 1,033 loans, paying $8,561.22 in tips and fees at an average APR of 1,539%.

This is not a Colorado story. The DC Attorney General sued EarnIn in 2024. The CFPB has examined the company's practices since 2019. Consumer complaints have been flowing into regulatory databases for years. The Colorado lawsuit is the latest chapter in a pattern that has been building for most of a decade.

EarnIn is not an anomaly. It is the logical endpoint of a fintech culture that treats compliance as an obstacle and vulnerable consumers as a revenue opportunity.

How the Product Actually Works

EarnIn markets its "Cash Out" product as accessing your own earned wages not a loan, just early access to money you've already made. No interest. No hidden fees. Just a tip, which the app presents as optional.

The complaint tells a different story.

EarnIn's app required multiple taps to select no tip. The no-tip pathway was buried. The app displayed emotionally manipulative messages "pay it forward" to steer users toward tipping. The tips went directly to EarnIn's bottom line, not to other users as the framing implied.

The result: consumers were charged finance charges that functioned as loan interest, without the disclosures that federal lending law requires. The Truth in Lending Act exists specifically to prevent this to ensure that borrowers understand the true cost of credit before they take it. EarnIn's position was that its product wasn't a loan. Colorado's AG's position is that the evidence says otherwise.

The 99.18% repayment rate on Colorado transactions is particularly telling. A "voluntary tip" that is paid on 99% of transactions is not voluntary. It is a fee, structured to look optional.

The Population Being Targeted

The Colorado complaint describes the typical EarnIn user: hourly workers, paycheck to paycheck, accessing their wages before payday because they cannot wait. The exact population that needs affordable credit most is the exact population being charged 1,421% APR.

This is not coincidental. It is structural.

A consumer with strong credit, savings, and financial options does not use an earned wage access app. EarnIn's entire business model depends on serving people who have no better alternative and then extracting maximum revenue from that captive audience through fees that are engineered to feel minimal on a per-transaction basis and devastating on an annualized one.

The consumer who took out 1,151 loans wasn't irrational. She was trapped. Each individual "Cash Out" probably felt manageable. The $4,038 in cumulative fees did not.

This is what predatory lending looks like when it wears a fintech logo and a friendly app design.

Why This Is a Stablecoin Story

The EarnIn case might seem like a story about earned wage access, consumer protection, and state AG enforcement. It is all of those things. It is also a stablecoin story.

Here's why.

The communities most harmed by high-cost credit products are the same communities that stablecoin platforms are best positioned to serve. The hourly worker in Denver who paid 1,421% APR to access her own wages. The family in Mexico City paying 6% to receive a remittance. The entrepreneur in Buenos Aires holding USDC because the peso can't hold value overnight. These are not different people. They are the same global population of people underserved by the traditional financial system and actively exploited by the products that fill the gap.

Stablecoin platforms that are serious about financial inclusion cannot stop at remittance. Remittance is the entry point. Credit is the next frontier.

Access to affordable, transparent, fairly priced credit is what separates a financial platform from a financial tool. A tool moves money. A platform builds financial lives.

What Credit Equity Actually Requires

The EarnIn case illustrates the failure mode of credit products that prioritize extraction over equity:

Transparency. The APR has to be disclosed, clearly, before the consumer takes the product. Not buried in terms of service. Not obscured by tip framing. On the screen, in plain language, before the tap that confirms the transaction.

Sustainable rates. A credit product at 1,421% APR is not serving the consumer. It is consuming them. The rate has to reflect the actual cost of risk, not the maximum the market will bear from people who have no alternatives.

No dark patterns. The "pay it forward" message that steered users toward tipping while the tip went to EarnIn not to other users is the kind of deceptive design that poisons the entire category. Every manipulative design choice in a credit product is a trust violation that the entire industry pays for.

Structural compatibility with compliance. A credit product that only works because it avoids the definition of a loan because calling it a loan would trigger disclosures that would make the product uncompetitive is a product that is structurally dependent on regulatory evasion. That's not a compliance program. That's the absence of one.
