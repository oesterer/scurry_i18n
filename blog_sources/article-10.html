Meet the Team: Why We Don't Do 996 at Scurry

October 2026 · Eric Kinney

We want to build the most athletic company in Silicon Valley.

That's not a metaphor. It's an operating philosophy and it shapes everything from how we structure our days to who we're looking to bring onto the team.

Let us explain.

Two Founders Who Have Been Around Long Enough to Know Better

Eric Kinney is 30+ years into a financial services career  BSA Officer, Certified Fraud Examiner, DOJ witness, LendingClub employee #7. Before the market opened this morning, he had already completed a two-hour open water swim or a two-hour run. Monday through Friday, every week, before 8am. Not because he has to. Because it's the foundation everything else is built on.

Andreas Oesterer has spent 20+ years leading global engineering organizations across fintech, cloud, and enterprise systems. After hours, he's building robots, flying 3D printed airplanes, tinkering with drones, and coaching the local high school robotics team. He has a family. He is present for them.

We are both, to put it plainly, old enough to have watched the 996 playbook fail spectacularly multiple times, in multiple companies, at enormous cost to everyone involved.

We built Scurry differently.

What "No 996" Actually Looks Like

It doesn't mean we work less. It means we work right.

Eric's calendar blocks two hours every weekday morning swim or run, completed before 8am, non-negotiable. By the time most Silicon Valley founders are hitting snooze for the second time, the day's first hard thing is already done. That discipline carries directly into how we approach the work: the compliance program was built before the product, the AML framework before the first user, the regulatory relationships before the growth metrics.

We have a daily team meeting. Short, direct, and we laugh a lot. Self-deprecation is a house rule. Two founders in their fifties building a stablecoin banking platform for emerging markets, pursuing an OCC National Bank charter, writing thrillers about financial crime on the side if you can't laugh at that picture, you're in the wrong company.

Andreas's tinkering philosophy of robots, drones, 3D printed aircraft, and high school robotics mentorship  is not separate from how he approaches the CTO role. It's the same mindset: build it, test it, break it, learn from it, build it better. No black boxes. No magic. Understand the system well enough to fix it when it fails.

This summer we brought in two interns juniors from UC Berkeley and UC Santa Barbara. They worked. They shipped. They were treated as contributors, not as people fetching coffee and waiting for permission. That's the culture we're building into the company from the start.

Why This Matters for a Compliance-First Company

Here's the thing about 996 culture and compliance that I've been writing about all summer: they are structurally incompatible.

The BSA Officer who is exhausted, under-resourced, and culturally dismissed who gets excluded from the product roadmap meeting and overridden on the SAR filing is the single point of failure between your institution and a fraud ring that has been mapping your controls for months. Compliance is not a feature you add when you have time. It's the foundation, or it's nothing.

At Scurry, the compliance program was operational before we acquired our first user. The AML program existed before the product did. The OCC charter process is underway before we've raised a seed round. That's not caution that's the only way to build financial infrastructure that survives contact with regulators, banking partners, and a market that is watching very carefully which stablecoin platforms can be trusted.

You cannot build that kind of institutional credibility with a team that's burning out on a 72-hour work week. You build it with people who have the energy, the clarity, and the discipline to make the right call under pressure — every time, not just when they're fresh.

Who We're Looking For

We're looking for athletic player-coaches.

Not athletes in the sense that you need to swim the Golden Gate or run ultras — though that's welcome. Athletic in the sense that you do hard things consistently, you know what it costs to train for something that matters, and you bring that orientation to the work.

Player-coaches because we're a small team building something significant. Everyone on the team does the work AND thinks about how the team gets better. No one is here just to manage. No one is here just to execute. Everyone does both.

We want people who:

— Have built something real and know what that actually requires — Understand that compliance and growth are not in opposition — Can laugh at themselves and the absurdity of what we're attempting — Have a life outside the office that makes them better at the work inside it — Want to be part of something that lasts, not something that burns bright and disappears

If you want to work 996, there are plenty of companies in this valley that will accommodate you. We are not one of them.

If you want to build something right, move far, and build something that lasts in a company where the CEO is done with his swim before you've had your first coffee we'd like to talk.

A Note on the Summer

This summer we had two UC system interns who helped us think through product, market, and compliance questions with fresh eyes. They were sharp, they were curious, and they contributed real work. We're grateful for them.

We also swam the Golden Gate, rode Kings Mountain, posted about fraud on Sandhill Road while wearing a Fraud Squad jersey, and filed our first OCC pre-application meeting notes.

Not bad for a company that doesn't do 996.
