Nobody Knows What a Stablecoin Is — And That's Our Problem to Solve

November 2026 · Eric Kinney

We have been spending a lot of time talking to businesses and consumers over the past several weeks. Customer discovery conversations. Operator interviews. The kind of conversations that exist to test your assumptions before you build too far in any one direction.

One finding has been consistent enough that it's worth naming directly:

Almost nobody knows what a stablecoin is.

Not in a dismissive way. Not in a way that suggests they don't care. In a way that suggests nobody has ever explained it to them in terms that connect to a problem they actually have.

What We're Hearing

The businesses we talk to have real cross-border payment problems. Moving money between the U.S. and Mexico is expensive. It's slow. The compliance burden is unclear. The exchange rate exposure is real. The operational complexity of managing payments across two currencies, two regulatory environments, and multiple banking relationships is significant for companies that just need to pay their suppliers on time.

These are not hypothetical problems. These are the problems keeping CFOs and operations managers up at night.

But when we describe stablecoin payment infrastructure as part of the solution, the conversation slows down. Not because people are resistant. Because they don't have the context to evaluate what we're describing.

What's the difference between a stablecoin and cryptocurrency? Is this the same thing as Bitcoin? What happens if the stablecoin de-pegs? Who regulates this? Is my money safe?

These are good questions. They deserve good answers. And the industry — broadly speaking — has done a poor job of providing them.

Why This Is Scurry's Problem to Solve

There's a version of this finding that reads as bad news: the market isn't ready, education takes time, stablecoin adoption is further away than the headlines suggest.

We read it differently.

The businesses we talk to have the payment problems. They are ready to solve those problems. What they don't have is a provider they trust to explain how the infrastructure works, why the compliance approach is sound, and what the actual risk profile looks like.

That's an education problem, and it's a trust problem. Both are solvable. And a compliance-first company — one that can walk a business through the regulatory framework, the BSA requirements, the GENIUS Act implications, and what "FinCEN-registered" actually means in practice — is positioned to solve them in a way that a technology-first company is not.

The stablecoin education gap is not an obstacle to Scurry's market. It is part of the market.

The businesses that understand how stablecoin payment infrastructure works — and trust the company explaining it — will move first. The rest will follow when the infrastructure is proven and the regulatory framework is clear.

We are building for the businesses that want to understand it now. The GENIUS Act, passed earlier this year, created the regulatory framework that makes that conversation possible. The compliance infrastructure we've built makes it credible.

What This Means for How We Work

Customer discovery is not just market research. It's the beginning of the education relationship we'll have with every customer we onboard.

The operator who doesn't know what a stablecoin is today is the same operator who will run their cross-border supplier payments on Scurry's infrastructure in twelve months — once they understand what the infrastructure does, how the compliance controls work, and why this is a better answer to the problem they already have.

That education starts in the discovery conversation. It continues through onboarding. It compounds over time.

Compliance-first isn't just an architecture choice. It's a communication strategy. We can explain exactly how this works, why it's safe, and what the regulatory framework says — because we built it that way from the beginning.
